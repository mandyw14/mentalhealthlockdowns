## Abstract
This study was conducted to better understand the public emotional response to the pandemic lockdowns, with a specific focus on the provinces within Canada. The social media platform, Twitter, was used to conduct sentiment analysis on COVID-related tweets. The OxCGRT stringency index was also used, which is an index developed by researchers at Oxford to capture the severity of public health measures globally and to quantify those into daily values for research purposes. Our results suggest that drastic, population-level policy changes and restrictions imposed in the first wave of COVID-19 were met favorably and equally by Canadians across all provinces according to the sentiment analysis conducted on COVID-related tweets but that across subsequent waves the public responses varied by province and wave. The results associated with wave 1 are consistent with other studies, which have been interpreted as reflecting a public relief that the government was responding appropriately to the threat of the virus. However, an examination over all 6 waves and how the emotional response was altered regionally had not been done before our study. Our findings demonstrate that emotional responses to public health measures are detectable using Twitter and could be useful in the future for ongoing monitoring.
